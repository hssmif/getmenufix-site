How to Price Your Menu on Uber Eats

Pricing on Uber Eats is not the same as pricing at your counter. Commission and packaging come out of every order, so a price that works in store can lose money on delivery. Here is a simple way to get it right.

Updated 10 October 2026

Quick answer

To price your menu on Uber Eats, start from your real cost per dish, add the commission and packaging you pay on each order, and set a delivery price that still leaves you a fair margin. Many restaurants charge a little more on delivery than in store; that's allowed, but be consistent and keep combos and sizes clear.

1. Know your real cost per dish

Start with what each dish costs you to make: ingredients, plus the container, lid, bag, sauce pots and cutlery that go with it. Packaging is easy to forget and adds up across a busy night. Write the total next to each of your best sellers.

2. Check the commission on your plan

Uber Eats takes a percentage of each order, and the rate depends on the plan you chose and whether you use their couriers or your own. Look it up in your agreement or in Uber Eats Manager rather than relying on what someone told you. Then, for each dish, work out what you actually keep after commission.

3. Decide on your delivery price

Many restaurants charge a bit more on delivery than in store to cover these costs. That's a normal business choice. A few tips:

  • Apply the same approach across the menu, so prices don't look random.
  • Round to clean prices, like $18.50 or $19, rather than odd cents.
  • Check your agreement for any conditions on price differences.
  • Be ready to explain it honestly if a regular asks.

4. Look again at dishes that don't travel well

Some dishes cost a lot to pack properly or arrive worse than they leave. If a dish barely makes money on delivery and gets mixed reviews, it may be better off in store only. A shorter delivery menu is also easier to choose from.

5. Use combos and sizes carefully

Combos can raise the value of each order, which helps when commission is a percentage. Make sure the combo is clearly a better deal than buying the items separately, and say exactly what's included: "Burger, chips and a can of drink". Sizes work the same way: "Large (serves 2)" helps people choose with confidence.

6. Price add ons and extras

Extra cheese, a second sauce or a side of avocado cost you money. Listing them as paid options in Uber Eats Manager is fair and common, and it lets customers build what they want.

7. Treat promotions as a test, not a habit

Discounts and free delivery offers can bring new customers, but they come out of your margin, and commission is still charged. Before running one, work out what you keep per order at the promotional price. Run one at a time, check the results, and stop it if it isn't paying off.

8. Review your prices regularly

Ingredient costs change. Check your delivery prices every few months, or whenever a key supplier puts prices up, so you're not quietly losing money on your best sellers.

Price is only part of the decision

People compare what they see. A clear photo, a name they understand and a description that says what's in the dish make a fair price feel like good value. Our guide to getting more orders covers the rest of your page.

Questions

Can I charge more on Uber Eats than in my shop?

Many restaurants set higher prices on delivery apps to cover commission and packaging. Check your current agreement with Uber Eats for any conditions, and keep the difference reasonable so regulars don't feel caught out.

How do I work out my Uber Eats price for a dish?

Add up the food cost and packaging, then work out what is left after the commission on your plan. If that leftover amount is less than you need to make the dish worth cooking, raise the price, change the portion or take it off delivery.

Should I offer promotions on Uber Eats?

Only if the numbers still work after the discount and the commission. A promotion that brings orders at a loss is not a win. Try one at a time and check the results in Uber Eats Manager.
