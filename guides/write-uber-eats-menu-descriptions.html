The formula: what it is, what's in it, why it's good

Most great descriptions follow three steps in one or two short sentences:

  1. What it is if the name doesn't say it already.
  2. The main ingredients, the ones people care about.
  3. One detail that makes it yours: how it's cooked, where it comes from, how it's served.

Uber's own advice is 140 to 260 characters, about two short sentences (Uber). Customers skim, so lead with the tastiest words.

Words that help, and words to avoid

Helpful: cooking methods (slow cooked, wood fired, crispy, chargrilled), textures (crunchy, silky, fudgy), freshness (made daily, baked in house), and clear portions (serves 2, 10 pieces).

Avoid: vague praise (delicious, amazing, best in town), ALL CAPS, internal codes, and claims you can't back up. Let the ingredients do the selling.

Don't forget the practical details

  • Spice level for hot dishes (mild, medium, hot).
  • Common dietary notes like vegetarian, vegan or gluten free, only if true.
  • What's included in combos and family meals.

Keep it honest

Describe the dish exactly as it arrives. If the photo or the words promise more than the plate delivers, customers notice, and that shows up in reviews.

Questions

How long should an Uber Eats menu description be?

Uber's own advice is 140 to 260 characters, about two short sentences. Long enough to say what's in the dish, short enough to skim.

What should a menu description include?

What the dish is, its main ingredients, and one detail that makes it yours. Add spice level, portion size and dietary notes when useful.

What words should I avoid in menu descriptions?

Vague praise like delicious or amazing, ALL CAPS, internal codes, and claims you can't back up.
