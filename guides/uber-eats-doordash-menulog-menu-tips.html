Uber Eats vs DoorDash vs Menulog: Menu Tips for Restaurants

If you're on Uber Eats, DoorDash and Menulog, you're really running three menus. The good news is that what makes a menu work is the same on all of them. Here is how to keep them consistent without doubling your workload.

Updated 10 October 2026

Quick answer

Uber Eats, DoorDash and Menulog all reward the same basics: real photos, clear names, short descriptions, best sellers first and accurate prices. Keep one master menu, adapt it to each app's editor and photo rules, and update all three whenever something changes.

What's the same on every app

Whichever app a customer opens, they decide the same way: they scroll, look at photos and read names. On all three, these basics matter most:

  • Real, bright photos of your best sellers.
  • Names people understand, with your signature name kept if you like.
  • A one line description of what's in each dish.
  • Short, logical categories with best sellers first.
  • Correct prices, sizes, hours and sold out items.

Keep one master menu

The easiest way to stay consistent is a single document or spreadsheet with every item's name, description, price, options and photo file. Make changes there first, then copy them into each app. This stops the common problem of a dish being removed on one app but still selling on another.

What differs between the apps

Each app has its own menu editor, its own photo rules and its own way of showing your page. Before you upload, check the current photo guidelines for each one: size, shape and what's allowed, like text or logos on the image. Crop from your original photos for each app rather than reusing an already cropped version.

Options, combos and add ons are also set up differently in each editor. Build them carefully on each app and place a test order if you can, so customers aren't charged the wrong amount or missing an item.

Fees and plans vary between apps and change over time. Look them up in your own agreements rather than relying on general comparisons, because what matters is what you actually pay.

Uber Eats tips

Uber Eats Manager lets you edit items, photos and categories, and add team members with menu access only. If items have no photo, Uber Eats may show photos taken by customers, so add your own to your best sellers. See our guide to editing your menu in Uber Eats Manager.

DoorDash tips

DoorDash menus are managed through the DoorDash Merchant Portal. As on every app, put your most ordered dishes first and give them photos. Check how your categories and options appear in the app after saving, because what looks right in the editor doesn't always look right on a phone.

Menulog tips

Menulog has been around for a long time and many regular customers still use it out of habit. Menu changes are made through its partner tools, and some changes may go through their team. Allow time for updates, and check your page once they're live.

Prices on more than one app

If the fees on your plans differ, you may choose different prices on each app. That's your call, but keep it simple: one clear rule per app is easier to manage than item by item differences. Our guide on how to price your menu on Uber Eats explains how to work out a delivery price.

Don't spread yourself too thin

Each app adds a tablet on the counter, a menu to update and orders to pack. Being on one app with a great, accurate page is better than three with old prices and missing photos. Add another app when you're confident you can keep it up to date.

Questions

Should I be on Uber Eats, DoorDash and Menulog at the same time?

Many restaurants are on more than one. It can reach more customers, but each app means another tablet, another menu to keep up to date and another set of fees. Start with what you can manage well.

Can I use the same menu on every delivery app?

Mostly, yes. Keep one master list of names, descriptions, prices and photos, then copy it into each app. Check each app's photo requirements and how it handles options and combos.

Can I have different prices on each app?

You can, if the fees on your plans differ. Check each agreement for conditions, and keep the differences simple so you can manage them.
