Uber Eats Photo Requirements and How to Take Great Food Photos on Your Phone

You don't need a photographer to have good photos on Uber Eats. With daylight, a clean background and a few simple habits, a phone is enough.

Uber Eats photo requirements

Every photo you upload in Uber Eats Manager is reviewed before it goes live. At the time of writing, Uber's merchant help pages ask for:

WhatRequirement
Shape5:4 (landscape)
Recommended size2880 × 2304 pixels (menu items and cover image)
FileJPG (PNG and GIF also accepted for items), up to 10 MB
ContentOne menu item per photo, centred, exactly as the customer receives it
Not allowedText, prices, logos or watermarks, people or faces (hands are fine), blurry photos

The app shows small square thumbnails, so keep the dish in the centre with some space around it. Uber can update these rules, so check the Uber Eats photo guidelines if a photo is rejected.

One more reason to add your own photos: when an item has no photo, Uber Eats can show photos that customers took themselves. Your own photos keep you in control of how your food looks.

Use daylight, not the kitchen lights

Kitchen lights are often yellow or green and make food look flat. Take your photos near a window during the day, with the light coming from the side. Turn the flash off: it makes food shiny and harsh.

Pick a simple background

A plain table, a wooden board or a light coloured surface works best. Move away bottles, cloths, receipts and anything else that isn't the dish. The food should be the only thing people look at.

Find the right angle for each dish

  • From above for flat dishes: pizza, bowls, platters, salads.
  • From a 45 degree angle for most plates and takeaway boxes.
  • From the side for tall food: burgers, layered cakes, drinks.

Fill the frame, and leave a little space

Get close so the dish fills most of the photo, with a small margin around it. Uber Eats crops photos into different shapes, so keep the food in the centre.

Show the real portion

Serve the dish exactly as customers receive it, in your usual packaging or plate. Photos that look bigger or fancier than the real thing lead to disappointed customers.

Keep a consistent style

Use the same background and angle for all your photos. A menu where every photo looks alike feels professional, even if each photo is simple.

Edit lightly

Your phone's built in editor is enough: a little more brightness, a little warmth, straighten the photo. Don't change the colours of the food or use heavy filters.

Start with your top ten

You don't need to photograph everything in one day. Your ten best sellers make the biggest difference, so start there.
